Museum of Lost Tech

A digital gallery dedicated to the forgotten media and hardware that paved our path.

3.5" Floppy Disk

Era: 1971 — 2011

The original physical save icon. A magnetic storage medium holding a maximum of 1.44 megabytes—scarcely enough for one modern photo.

Technical Specs:

•Max Capacity: 1.44 MB

•Transfer Speed: ~50 KB/s

•Status: Replaced by USB Drives

56k Dial-Up Modem

Era: 1990s

a legacy hardware device that connects a computer to the internet or another computer using a standard analog telephone line at speeds up to 56Kbps.

Technical Specs:

•Top Bandwidth: 56 Kbit/s

•Media Type: Analog Phone Lines

•Status: Replaced by Broadband & Fiber

Audio Cassette Tape

Era: 1963 — 2000s

The portable music revolution before MP3s. Famous for tangling magnetic tape internally, which usually required a pencil to manually re-spool.

Technical Specs:

•Runtime: 60 to 90 minutes

•Mechanism: Magnetic Ribbon

•Status: Replaced by CDs & MP3s

Nintendo Game Boy

Era: 1989 — 2003

The 8-bit handheld console that revolutionized mobile gaming. Powered by 4 AA batteries and featured an un-backlit olive green dot-matrix screen.

Technical Specs:

•Resolution: 160 x 144 pixels

•Power: 4x AA Batteries (~30 hrs)

•Status: Replaced by modern handhelds

CRT TV & Monitor

Era: 1950s — 2000s

Heavy cathode-ray tube displays that dominated homes for decades. Known for their glowing glass screens, buzzing frequencies, and deep depth profile.

Technical Specs:

•Display Type: Electron Gun & Phosphor

•Weight: 30 - 100+ lbs

•Status: Replaced by LCD & OLED screens

Netscape Navigator

Era: 1994 — 2008

The pioneer web browser of the 1990s. Introduced JavaScript to the world and held 90% market share before losing the historic first 'Browser War'.

Technical Specs:

•Peak Market Share: 90%+ (mid-90s)

•Legacy Tech: Created JavaScript & SSL

•Status: Replaced by Firefox & Chrome

LaserDisc

Era: 1978 — 2000s

The giant optical disc that brought high-quality video home before DVDs. Disc size was equivalent to a full vinyl record and often required flipping.

Technical Specs:

•Disc Diameter: 12 inches (30 cm)

•Video Signal: Analog (425+ lines resolution)

•Status: Replaced by DVD & Blu-ray

PalmPilot (PDA)

Era: 1996 — 2010

The precursor to the modern smartphone. A handheld organizer using a monochrome touchscreen and a stylus to take handwritten notes using 'Graffiti' script.

Technical Specs:

•Memory: 512 KB to 1 MB RAM

•Input Method: Stylus & Touch Screen

•Status: Replaced by Smartphones
